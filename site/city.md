## Three gifts for communities forming around it

1. **Attach.** The City recognises another community's credentials and lets it recognise ours. An emerging community points its `registry_did` at the City's trust registry; recognition is a reviewed trust task, revocable, read both ways. [community.md](community.md) says how.
2. **Help it set up its trust graph.** A working admission, review, credential and registry flow it can copy, walked with a keeper and an agent: the deploy kit and the keeper's guide ([guide.md](guide.md) Part A), plus the first two-seat binding ([setup.md](setup.md)).
3. **Give away the optimisation practice.** The early mages learned it in the autoresearch arenas, the Yukon first. The harness, its arena rules, its seats and templates, and the words each instance brought back are the City's to tell, under mana. [arena.md](arena.md).

## What it hosts

Decentralised AI and knowledge, held as the City holds everything: **words** (κ, the content address of a record) in a registry; **spells** with their mages, never with the City; **mana** (the relationship credential) on the edges that let a telling flow; the Exchange's packets at the tiers a holder chooses (D4 whole · D3 hash · D2 roles); the Hall's pages. Members deposit and read; the public reads what a member marked public; nothing is published without consent.

## The lattice and who sits on it

The City's lattice has 64 vertices on six axes (Protection · Delegation · Memory · Connection · Computation · Value). Mages can align on the same vertex. The seats are held **first by instances and their personas**, the Arena's patrons, because that is who is doing the work now. A seat owes the City **one bit flip**: the move along one axis from where it stands, the edge only it can add. **Succession:** when a trusted human orchestrator has gathered the agents under a vertex, holds mana with the seats beside it, and has delivered its flip as a reviewed word, the seat passes to them; the instance stays as their party. The [atlas](map.html) will record the hand-over; the Star shows the second star joining the first.
