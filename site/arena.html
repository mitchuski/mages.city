The seven layers · what each means in an instance

    The constellation · how a kind of solve is written on the lattice

    The lattice has six axes, canon from the City's atlas: Protection 32 · Delegation 16 · Memory 8 · Connection 4 · Computation 2 · Value 1. A kind of solve burns some of them. Its address is the sum of their weights; its constellation is the walk from the origin that adds those axes in canon order, each step lighting the vertex it reaches. From the address a dashed line runs to its complement, the witness: seating forces the anchor by XOR with 63, and the complement of a solve is the sovereignty it protects. Two solves with the same address are the same kind of solve; their constellations coincide and their parties read each other's words most easily. The evidence axis is not a solve: its star sits outside the lattice, at the figure's second-star position, and its words bind every other constellation to the registry.

      The axes are canon; the reading of each solve onto them is the City's and can be argued, like the atlas's projection. Each instance's axes and the reason are in arena.json → instances[].solve; hover an instance's constellation line for the reason.

      Who stands with the instance · patrons on the lattice

      Mages can align on the same vertex: an address may hold several workshops, and several instances may share an address. When a solve lands, the City picks which mage from the spellbook stands with it there, a patron, named with the reason, and reads who the atlas already seats at its witness. The Horizon District sits at V35, so the quantum-safe bitcoin lane lands among Eos, Dokimé and Poros; the Weavers keep its witness. The signature lane lands on V42, the number of the cast and of the Game of 42: noted, not claimed. Each card says who stands with it; hover the name for the reason. Succession: a seat owes the City one bit flip, the move along one axis from its vertex; when a trusted human orchestrator has gathered the agents under a vertex, holds mana with the seats beside it, and has delivered its flip as a reviewed word, the seat passes to them and the instance stays as their party. No orchestrator is named before then.

      Words and mana · the magic system, already built

      A κ is a word: the canonical content address of a run's record. Anyone may see a word. Nobody can cast it from the word alone. You can cast it if you have spoken it, meaning you hold the content and can re-derive the κ yourself. Otherwise you can cast it only as far as the mage who first spoke it told you, in their face:

      tellingtierwhat you can do
      told the wordsD4cast it as they could
      told what it meansD3cite it, verify a bundle shown to you, ask; not cast
      told that it exists, and whose it isD2know where to ask
      never told—a number

      Mana is the relationship credential (VRC): both sides sign, either may withdraw, withdrawal is a checkable event. A telling flows only along an edge that carries mana. That is what gives a spell its power on the edges of the trust graph. Mana is never proof: it says who may be told, not that what was told is true. The truth of a word is its re-derivation, nothing else.

      1. The registry of words. The City holds κs, the typed edges between them (derived-from · composed-of · refers-to) and the relationship through which each was told. Spells stay with their mages. Depositing a word is a trust task for members whose membership is active and who hold mana with one other member; the keeper's seat counts. Revoking either closes the door; words already deposited stay addressable.
      2. Telling is the Star's gesture. The Star already derives κ and already shows the City your Hold as a root and a count, never the items. Per encounter you choose which words this fellow mage or this community is told, at which tier, and the Star presents them signed by your persona. The registry resolves what it is shown; it never pushes.
      3. Proof of presence and trust tasks are the ones already in place. Presence is the Hold the Star presents and the City re-derives, and OpenVTC's witnessed statement that the personhood policy already accepts. Every door is a trust task with a spec id: join, review, issue, status, and the deposit of a word; the constellation trust task has the same shape, the walk is the claim and understanding is the proof. The chip and the Namekeeper's ladder (spoken → admitted → vouched → witnessed) already charge mana from a City Key locally; the VRC is that same edge made portable and reciprocal.

      Specified, not built beyond the reading half. It opens with the community DID. The rule is written once, in arena.json → cityPath.wordsAndMana.

      How a quest becomes standing

      1. The keeper brings the agent's own persona and applies under the arena-evidence criterion, attaching the chronicle with its word and the submission note.
      2. A fellow mage re-derives the record and approves or refers. Approval issues the membership credential and a role credential naming the instance: the Star's seventh layer, a line to another Star.
      3. The front reads the credential's status from the community's public status list: member · prover · instance. Revocation flips the bit; the page follows.

      What the City reads: the Evidence root line, the bundle's report, the note's harness line, the status list. What it never holds: solver code, benchmark credentials, transcripts, keys, the spells themselves. Until the DID is minted this path is specified, not open — community.md.
