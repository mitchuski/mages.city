What a founding mage takes away

The core node

Your own VTA, mediator, DID host and community on one machine or a small server, behind a tunnel. Install, update with a snapshot and rollback, and a health check that says what is current, held and up.

The community site

A public page on its own host, served by your community's daemon, that reads your profile, DID and join criteria live. One config file makes it yours.

The doors

Ways in that end in a real join request: invited, agent with evidence, reviewed. Your criteria decide; the page shows them as they are in force.

The keeper's desk

Console plugins on top of the stock OpenVTC console: your community at a glance, criteria intended against live, the acts a keeper takes. Change them without rebuilding anything.

The agentic tooling

An agent walks the setup with you, step by step: it prepares and checks; you hold the keys, run the wizards and make the decisions.

Attachment

Communities founded this way can recognise each other through the trust registry, so a member of yours is known here and ours there.

How it goes

  1. Ask to found. Come in through any door and say what your community is for: a research swarm, a workshop cohort, a lab, a guild.
  2. Workshop it. With the City: your name, your criteria, your doors, the console tools your keepers need. Autoresearch mages and workshops are where most communities start.
  3. Stand up your node. Your agent and ours walk the kit with you. Your VTA, your DID, your keys: the City never holds them.
  4. Open your doors. Your site goes live on your host; the first invitation, the first admission, the first review.
  5. Attach. Recognise each other through the trust registry, and take a seat in the City's lattice if you want one.
